## Overview

Realistic and real-time surgical simulators play an increasingly important role in surgical robotics research,
such as surgical robot learning and automation, and surgical skills assessment.
The ability to simulate diverse objects and contact-rich manipulation tasks, such as tissue cutting and blood suction,
is important because they commonly arise in surgery. CRESSim enables simulation of surgical tasks involving different instruments,
soft tissue, and body fluids, and incorporates the da Vinci Research Kit (dVRK) console and master tool manipulators (MTMs)
for virtual-reality teleoperation.

Our latest engine, CRESSim-Neo, is a batched GPU position-based dynamics (PBD) engine for rigid bodies, deformable tissue, fluids, and strands,
with batched rendering, surgery-specific sensing, and a GPU-resident data pipeline for scalable surgical robot learning.

If you find this project helpful for your research, please consider citing one or more of the papers listed below.
